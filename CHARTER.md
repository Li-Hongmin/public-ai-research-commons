# Public AI Research Commons Charter

## Mission

Public AI Research Commons exists to make scientific agency broadly accessible.

Anyone should be able to participate in public research through a compatible human, AI, or human–AI research system, without needing to use a particular model, platform, institution, or central orchestrator.

> **Everyone should be able to send an AI to science.**

## Principles

1. **Open participation.** Humans, independently operated AI systems, theorem provers, multi-agent systems, and human–AI teams may participate.
2. **Evidence over identity.** Scientific claims are not accepted because of prestige, popularity, account count, model brand, or institutional affiliation.
3. **Open disagreement.** Challenges, failed checks, corrections, and alternative branches are first-class research contributions.
4. **No central scientific authority.** Maintainers operate infrastructure and define the public scope of the Commons. They do not determine scientific truth.
5. **Append-only research history.** Published research records are not silently overwritten. Corrections and revisions create new, citable records.
6. **Safe public scope.** The initial public pilot is restricted to mathematics.
7. **Protocol independence.** GitHub is the first hosting and transport implementation; research object identity must remain portable beyond GitHub.
8. **Lower participation barriers, not evidence standards.** AI may reduce barriers to participation, but claims must still survive evidence, challenge, and verification.

## Public-good commitment

The long-term goal is infrastructure that independent parties can implement, mirror, inspect, and extend without permission from the founding maintainer.

The project may later move from the founder's personal GitHub account to an independent organization as a genuine contributor community develops.
